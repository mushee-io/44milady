# 44 Milady — Milestone 8: Repayment + complete lifecycle

Milestone 8 completes the healthy lending loop.

## Repayment instructions

### `repay_usdg(amount)`

The borrower repays a specific amount of synchronized USDG debt.

Flow:

1. accrue pool interest;
2. synchronize the borrower's debt against the global borrow index;
3. validate `0 < amount <= debt`;
4. transfer USDG from borrower wallet to the pool vault;
5. reduce borrower debt;
6. reduce pool total borrowed;
7. refresh utilization and APR cache;
8. update borrower health.

### `repay_usdg_max()`

The borrower repays the **entire synchronized debt balance**, including accrued interest.

There is no frontend estimate trusted by the program: the amount is resolved on-chain after interest synchronization.

### `repay_usdg_on_behalf(amount)`

A third-party wallet may repay another borrower's debt without taking ownership of the collateral or credit account.

The payer signs and supplies USDG. The borrower does not sign.

### `repay_usdg_on_behalf_max()`

A third party may clear the borrower's entire synchronized debt.

This is useful for sponsored repayment, treasury support, rescue flows and future liquidation-related tooling.

## Pause semantics

Repayment remains enabled when the protocol is paused.

A pause may stop risk-increasing actions such as new borrowing, supplying or collateral withdrawal, but it should not stop users from reducing debt.

## Liquidity restoration

Every USDG repaid returns physical tokens to the canonical pool vault and simultaneously reduces `total_borrowed_usdg`.

Therefore:

```
available liquidity after repay
  = available liquidity before repay + repayment
```

This is what makes accrued supplier yield and protocol reserves progressively realizable as borrowers repay.

## Health improvement

Repayment cannot make a position riskier.

After repayment:

```
health factor = liquidation capacity / remaining debt
```

If debt reaches zero, the stored health factor becomes `u64::MAX` (no debt).

## Overpayment protection

Partial repayment rejects:

- zero amount;
- amount greater than synchronized debt;
- wallets without enough USDG.

This prevents accidental donations to the lending pool through the repayment instruction.

## Full borrower lifecycle

A borrower can now execute:

```
create credit account
→ deposit collateral
→ borrow USDG
→ accrue interest
→ partial repay
→ repay MAX
→ withdraw all collateral
→ close empty collateral vaults
→ close credit account
```

### `close_collateral_vault`

After a market's collateral balance has been completely withdrawn, its token vault can be closed and the rent returned to the owner.

The instruction requires:

- vault token amount = 0;
- the market no longer appears in the credit account collateral vector.

### `close_credit_account`

The PDA can only close when:

- debt = 0;
- no collateral entries remain.

The account rent is returned to the owner.

## Supplier lifecycle

A supplier can:

```
supply USDG
→ earn indexed yield
→ withdraw full balance
→ close supplier position
```

Before `close_supplier_position`, the program accrues the pool and synchronizes the supplier index. This prevents closing a seemingly-zero stale position that actually earned yield.

## M8 acceptance flow

1. Wallet A supplies 10,000 USDG.
2. Wallet B deposits collateral and borrows 3,000 USDG.
3. Interest accrues.
4. Wallet B repays 1,000 USDG.
5. Pool available liquidity increases by exactly 1,000 USDG.
6. Wallet C repays 500 USDG on behalf of Wallet B.
7. Wallet B calls MAX repay and clears the remaining synchronized debt.
8. Wallet B withdraws every collateral asset.
9. Wallet B closes each empty collateral vault.
10. Wallet B closes the credit-account PDA.
11. Wallet A withdraws its complete supplier claim when liquidity permits and closes its SupplierPosition.
